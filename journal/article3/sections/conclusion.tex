\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether a single-pass callee-precondition propagation, the
strategy used by an established heuristic JML inferrer, captures
everything a top-down compositional refinement would. We implemented
the refinement as a 414-line analyser and measured its effect on
five real-world libraries spanning four idiom families
(21{,}052 inferred methods in total). The single pass does
\emph{not} subsume on any library studied: compositional refinement
adds at least one new clause to 24--44\% of methods in each
library's cache (median 36.33\%), for a total of 42{,}370 new
precondition clauses and 25{,}203 new postcondition clauses across
the five corpora.

The shape distribution explains the gap. Branch-conditional
implications (\texttt{guard ==> obligation}) make up the largest
single shape on four libraries of five (34--64\% of additions);
disjunctive obligations from polymorphic dispatch are present on
every library and peak at 9.7\% on Vavr. Both shapes arise from
contexts the single pass cannot see: enclosing branch guards and
sibling dispatch candidates. The WP-style walk through the body
that the second pass performs is what supplies the missing
context. Null checks and range bounds account for most of the
remainder.

The runtime cost of the second pass is small: 44 seconds total across
all five libraries (21{,}052 methods), with no fixpoint iteration
triggered at any library's scale. The refactoring is therefore a
clear value: an inferrer-pipeline addition of seconds yields a
measurably richer spec set for downstream consumers.

What this paper does not measure is whether the additional clauses
materially improve downstream tooling --- whether tests generated
from the refined specs catch more bugs, whether IDEs displaying the
refined contracts help reviewers, whether LLMs reading them produce
better completions. Those questions are the planned next stage of
this thesis programme; the present article establishes the
structural prerequisite, that the additional clauses exist, before
the downstream-impact question can be meaningfully posed.

Two bugs were uncovered in the analyser by the corpus-scale run
(both string-splitting errors that the unit-test inputs did not
exercise); we report them as a methodological note. The analyser
ships with the corpus-scale measurement harness as a runnable
replication artefact.
