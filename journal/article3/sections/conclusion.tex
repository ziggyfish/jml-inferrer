\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether a single-pass callee-precondition propagation, the
strategy used by an established heuristic JML inferrer, captures
everything a top-down compositional refinement would. We implemented
the refinement as a 410-line analyser and measured its effect on two
real-world libraries. The single pass does \emph{not} subsume:
compositional refinement adds at least one new clause to 43.85\% of
Commons Lang methods and 36.33\% of Commons IO methods, for a total
of 7{,}764 new precondition clauses and 7{,}221 new postcondition
clauses across the two corpora.

The shape distribution explains the gap. Branch-conditional
implications (\texttt{guard ==> obligation}) make up 53\% of the
additions on Commons Lang and 43\% on Commons IO. These clauses
arise from calls inside branch bodies whose enclosing guard must be
lifted into the caller's precondition. A single-pass analyser that
appends the callee's substituted precondition unconditionally
cannot produce the \texttt{guard ==>} prefix; the WP-style walk
through the body that the second pass performs is what supplies
the missing context. Null checks and range bounds account for most
of the remainder; polymorphic-dispatch disjunctions account for a
small but visible 2--4\%.

The runtime cost of the second pass is small: 3.5 seconds on a
3{,}494-method library, with no fixpoint iteration triggered at the
libraries' scale. The refactoring is therefore a clear value: an
inferrer-pipeline addition of seconds yields a measurably richer
spec set for downstream consumers.

What this paper does not measure is whether the additional clauses
materially improve downstream tooling --- whether tests generated
from the refined specs catch more bugs, whether IDEs displaying the
refined contracts help reviewers, whether LLMs reading them produce
better completions. Those questions are the planned next stage of
this thesis programme; the present article establishes the
structural prerequisite, that the additional clauses exist, before
the downstream-impact question can be meaningfully posed.

Two bugs were uncovered in the analyser by the corpus-scale run
(both string-splitting errors that the unit-test inputs did not
exercise); we report them as a methodological note. The analyser
ships with the corpus-scale measurement harness as a runnable
replication artefact.
