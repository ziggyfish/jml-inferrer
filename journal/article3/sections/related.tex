\subsection{Position within the inference / verification spectrum}

Table~\ref{tab:novelty} positions the present work against its
closest neighbours along three axes: whether the technique
\emph{produces} JML, \emph{reasons compositionally} across procedure
boundaries, and has that reasoning SMT-discharged. The cell our pass
occupies --- produces JML, reasons compositionally via WP lifting, no
sound discharge --- is unoccupied by any prior tool to our knowledge.

The novelty is the combination, not any individual axis. KeY and
OpenJML SC perform the compositional reasoning we do but do not
\emph{produce} the specification --- they consume one written by hand;
the predecessor inferrer~\cite{edmonds2026inference} produces JML at
this scale but propagates single-pass, so it cannot synthesise the
branch-guarded or polymorphic
shapes that account for 39--62\% of our new clauses
(Table~\ref{tab:shapes}); Infer is genuinely compositional but does
not target JML. The gap is narrow but real: between
\emph{rich-by-construction} and \emph{rich-by-hand-and-tool-chain} JML.

\paragraph{Compositional reasoning frameworks.}
The classic foundations are Hoare's axiomatic semantics~\cite{hoare1969axiomatic}, Dijkstra's weakest-precondition calculus~\cite{dijkstra_guarded_1975,dijkstra1976discipline}, and Cousot \& Cousot's abstract interpretation~\cite{cousot1977abstract}, which frames our second pass as narrowing a fixpoint via callee summaries. Boogie's verification-condition generator~\cite{barnett2005weakest} performs the full WP transformation our scaffold approximates, but targets verification rather than the cheap-and-rich JML emission this work serves.

\paragraph{Procedure summaries and interprocedural analysis.}
Infer's~\cite{calcagno2015infer} bi-abduction summaries in separation
logic are already compositional in the first pass;
Houdini~\cite{flanagan2001houdini} conjectures candidate annotations
and prunes them via ESC/Java, a test-and-refute loop closer in spirit
than mechanism; Daikon's dynamic invariants~\cite{ernst2007daikon}
have no analogue of a second pass.

\paragraph{Specification inference for JML.}
The immediate predecessor is~\cite{edmonds2026inference}, which introduced the heuristic JML inferrer we extend, and~\cite{edmonds2026embedding}, which addressed distribution (embedding inferred specs in bytecode and OpenAPI descriptions). PreInfer~\cite{preinfer2017} uses dynamic symbolic execution (DSE) to infer preconditions, trace-driven rather than syntactic. Jdoctor~\cite{jdoctor2018}, Toradocu~\cite{toradocu2016}, and Pandita et al.~\cite{pandita2012inferring} mine specifications from natural-language documentation and do not propagate. A more recent line uses LLMs to synthesise contracts or oracles directly~\cite{endres2024can,ma2024specgen,richter2025beyondpostconditions,konstantinou2024oracles}; those are complementary --- whatever produces the per-method spec, our refinement pass can be applied to the cache afterwards.

\paragraph{Bytecode rewriting and analysis frameworks.}
ASM~\cite{asm}, Byte Buddy~\cite{bytebuddy}, Javassist~\cite{chiba2000javassist}, Soot~\cite{vallee1999soot}, and AspectJ~\cite{kiczales1997aspectj} operate at the bytecode or analysis layer, not compositional spec inference; our analyser works at the source-AST layer.

\paragraph{Test generation that consumes contracts.}
Korat~\cite{boyapati2002korat} generates inputs from \texttt{@Requires} predicates; EvoSuite~\cite{fraser2011evosuite} and Randoop~\cite{pacheco2007randoop} consume contracts and observed exceptions as oracles; TOGA~\cite{dinella2022toga} and TOGLL~\cite{hossain2024togll} are neural oracle generators. Richer per-method specifications, such as those our refinement pass produces, improve input quality for all of these.

\paragraph{Polymorphic dispatch in static analysis.}
Tip et al.~\cite{tip2002practical} address polymorphic dispatch via
class-hierarchy analysis (CHA) and rapid-type analysis (RTA), the
techniques the inferrer's \texttt{CallGraph} uses to enumerate
candidate callees. Our disjunction-over-candidates is the static
equivalent of summarising the runtime-may-dispatch set, subject to
the standard CHA trade-off: many candidates yield an easy-to-satisfy,
less informative disjunction.
