\chapter*{Publications During Candidature}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Publications During Candidature}
\markboth{Publications}{Publications}

This thesis is presented as a traditional monograph: a single continuous
argument, with one unified review of the literature and one account of the
methods, into which the research has been integrated rather than assembled from
separately published chapters. The material has nonetheless been developed
through three manuscripts, listed below, whose results are incorporated and
substantially expanded within the unified narrative. In each case the candidate
(B.\ Edmonds) was responsible for the conception of the study, the design and
implementation of the software artefacts, the design and execution of the
experiments, the analysis of the results, and the writing; the associated
supervisor (M.\ Utting) provided guidance on study design, framing, and
presentation.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item B.\ Edmonds and M.\ Utting. ``Do Specifications Help Large Language
        Models Write Better Unit Tests? An Empirical Study with Inferred JML
        Contracts.'' \emph{Manuscript prepared for submission to the Journal
        of Software: Evolution and Process (JSEP).}

  \item B.\ Edmonds and M.\ Utting. ``Embedding Inferred JML Contracts in
        Java Bytecode and OpenAPI Specifications.'' \emph{Manuscript prepared
        for submission to a software-engineering conference.}

  \item B.\ Edmonds and M.\ Utting. ``Compositional Propagation of Inferred
        Specifications and Its Use for Library Compatibility.'' \emph{Manuscript
        in preparation.}
\end{enumerate}

The research reported in these manuscripts is distributed across the thesis
according to the argument's structure rather than confined to one chapter
apiece: the inference engine and its accuracy underpin
Chapters~\ref{ch:inference} and~\ref{ch:testgen}, the distribution mechanism
Chapter~\ref{ch:distribution}, and the compositional propagation and its
compatibility consequence Chapter~\ref{ch:compositional}.
